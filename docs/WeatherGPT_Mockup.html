General Chat

Persona: everyone · baseline layer
DOES

Natural-language Q&A in English + Indian languages, voice in/out, proactive alert banners, and — the signature detail — every answer carries a visible source chip showing exactly which data tier produced it.

WHY IT EXISTS

Satisfies the PS's literal brief as a general conversational platform. It's also the shared foundation every other screen is built on top of.

BETTER THAN EXISTING TOOLS

Unlike a regular weather app, any phrasing works. Unlike ChatGPT's web search, the source is never a black box — and it pushes the cyclone-warning banner before you ask.

WHERE IT HONESTLY LACKS

On a single one-off question like the first bubble above, a generic LLM with browsing does nearly as well. This screen alone is not the pitch — it's necessary, not sufficient.

Farmer Advisory

Persona: farmer · flagship differentiator
DOES

Persistent crop profile, an 8-crop curated rule table (sowing windows, thresholds, pest triggers), a computed Crop Risk Index weighted against a real historical baseline, a harvest-timing advisory, and free-text crop Q&A grounded in the rule table rather than the model's general training.

WHY IT EXISTS

This is the concrete proof of the whole differentiation argument. A chatbot can say "it might rain" — it can't say "your wheat is at grain-filling stage, harvest before Thursday," unless someone built that logic and fed it in.

BETTER THAN EXISTING TOOLS

ChatGPT has no persistent profile and no cited source for farming advice. Government portals (Kisan Suvidha etc.) are form-heavy, not conversational, and not personalized to one field's exact stage.

WHERE IT HONESTLY LACKS

8 crops with general thresholds — not hyper-local soil/variety nuance a real agronomist would weigh. This is decision support, not a replacement for an extension officer, and should be pitched that way.

Trip Planner

Persona: traveler · moderate-depth feature
DOES

Turns a whole trip into one coherent, synthesized answer instead of seven separate lookups — and explicitly labels whether each day is a real forecast or a historical estimate.

WHY IT EXISTS

Demonstrates the system reasoning across a time range, not just answering isolated facts — a genuinely different interaction shape from "what's the weather."

BETTER THAN EXISTING TOOLS

Weather apps hand you 7 days of raw numbers and leave the synthesis to you. A generic chatbot might synthesize too — but won't reliably flag when it's quietly guessing past the forecast horizon.

WHERE IT HONESTLY LACKS

Closer to "smart formatting of existing data" than genuinely novel logic. It's a real feature, but the lightest-weight one here — first to trim if time runs short.

Disaster Manager View

Persona: disaster manager / govt. stakeholder
DOES

Aggregates currently active official alerts across regions into one scannable list — same alerting backend as the general chat, a different lens on top of it.

WHY IT EXISTS

The PS explicitly names disaster managers and government agencies as served users — this is the concrete UI proof that persona is actually built for, not just claimed in a slide.

BETTER THAN EXISTING TOOLS

Today this kind of view means manually checking several regional bulletins. This consolidates them, transparently sourced, in one place.

WHERE IT HONESTLY LACKS

The team has no real access to how an actual disaster manager works day to day — this is our least user-validated screen, built on a reasonable guess rather than real workflow research.

Historical Analytics

Persona: farmer + general analyst · secondary view
DOES

Filterable charts and trend lines over the preloaded historical dataset — rainfall, temperature, humidity, by location and date range.

WHY IT EXISTS

Makes the PS's "climate trend analysis" feature genuinely explorable, and is what feeds the Crop Risk Index's baseline-deviation term on the Farmer screen.

BETTER THAN EXISTING TOOLS

Most consumer weather apps don't expose historical trend exploration at all — this makes the data visually interactive instead of requiring the LLM to describe it in prose.

WHERE IT HONESTLY LACKS

Purely exploratory — it doesn't drive a decision on its own the way the Risk Index does. Easiest screen to simplify if time gets tight.

Offline Mode

Applies across every screen · connectivity layer
DOES

Cache-first app shell (opens instantly on weak signal), cached profile/forecast/alerts in local storage, a template-based offline responder answering common questions with no network at all, and Background Sync queuing anything that genuinely needs the live LLM.

WHY IT EXISTS

Rural connectivity is mostly intermittent 2G/3G, not a clean online/offline switch — this is the honest, buildable answer to that reality, and it's the PS's own "rural accessibility" language made concrete.

BETTER THAN EXISTING TOOLS

A generic chatbot app fails outright with no connection. Most weather apps show a blank error. This stays genuinely useful for the common questions, and never pretends to answer something it can't.

WHERE IT HONESTLY LACKS

This is not true internet independence — a live LLM call always needs a network round trip eventually. We're claiming graceful degradation, not magic. For zero-smartphone users, only a demo-mockup SMS channel exists, not live infrastructure (see Roadmap).

A small in-browser model (e.g. via WebLLM) could in principle answer basic queries with truly zero network. Real research risk in a 12-day window — flag it as future work in the pitch rather than committing engineering time to it now.

Worth exploring, not promising

SMS query/response channel

The only channel that reaches a user with no smartphone at all. Real sending isn't free at volume, so keep this as a demo-only mockup of the flow, not live infrastructure — mentioning it as designed-for-but-intentionally-not-built is a stronger, more honest signal than building a fragile version.

The throughline across this whole list: everything that deepens Farmer Advisory or the connectivity story is worth real time. Everything that just adds breadth to the general chat layer is not — that's the difference between standing out and building a nicer version of what every other team will also submit.
